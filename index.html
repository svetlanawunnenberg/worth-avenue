The Arcade Tradition

Burlington Arcade, London

Piccadilly · opened 20 March 1819

Lord George Cavendish, later the first Earl of Burlington, commissioned the architect Samuel Ware to roof a narrow lane beside Burlington House and line it with shops. The stated purpose was elegant; the private one was practical, since he wanted to stop passers-by tossing oyster shells over his garden wall. What opened in 1819 became one of Britain's first covered shopping arcades and the model for every one that followed.

It is still policed by its own beadles, recruited originally from Cavendish's own regiment, the 10th Royal Hussars, and still dressed in frock coats and gold-braided top hats. Running, whistling and singing remain forbidden â whistling because it was once the pickpockets' signal that a beadle was coming.

That is the lineage Burlington Arcade at 150 Worth Avenue draws on: a covered street of small shopfronts, daylit from above, held to a standard of behaviour and finish that a department store floor plate cannot produce.

Worth Avenue

Where Palm Beach Learned to Build

Palm Beach, Florida · 1918 to today

In 1918 Addison Mizner arrived in Palm Beach at the invitation of Paris Singer, the sewing-machine heir, who commissioned him to build a club on what was then a sand track. The Everglades Club opened in February 1919, pink-stuccoed and Moorish-inflected, and it set the terms for everything after it.

Mizner's instinct was not to design a building but to imply a history. He wanted a Mediterranean village that looked as though it had accumulated over centuries, mixing motifs across countries and eras so that a structure finished in 1919 already read as a hundred years old. In 1923 he cut Via Mizner through the block behind the club â a pedestrian lane of shopfronts, courtyards and balconies, terracotta barrel tile over white stucco, Venice by way of Spain.

The Vias are the point. Worth Avenue works because it is not one frontage but a series of intimate passages cut through depth, and that is precisely the move Burlington Arcade makes at 150 Worth: a covered street through a building that had turned its back on the idea.
